# Rank 15: controller-authentic raised-hand ride

## Result

The injected jump-kick predecessor is no longer needed for the local Rank-15 hand ride.  On an authentic North-American ROM, Mario was first accepted only after the game itself reported the real closed Eyerok hand as both his floor owner and platform.  A had already been held continuously since Area 3 entry.  At hand timer 2 the probe made no Mario-state write and supplied one new B edge; the retail action loop performed `ACT_IDLE → ACT_PUNCHING → ACT_JUMP_KICK` in one frame, with internal input `0x20a0` (`A_DOWN` and `B_PRESSED`, but no `A_PRESSED`).  Mario rose from Y `-1228` to `-1208` with remaining vertical velocity `16`, reached `-1192` with velocity `12`, caught the hand's first `+85` step at the ordinary 49-unit floor gap, and stayed attached through `+70,+55,+40,+25,+10`, ending at Y `-943`.

## What this settles

This is a real controller predecessor from the accepted local contact boundary: the probe no longer writes Mario's action, action state, velocity, position, or facing on the release poll.  Generated US and JP Clight syntax also contains the matching chain: idle's B branch selects punching, punching state zero tests held A and selects jump-kick, and `execute_mario_action` loops across the stationary and object-action dispatchers in one frame.  The checked Coq receipt is in `proofs/EyerokRank15ControllerRide.v`; the exact concise trace is `../../old-proofs/eyerok-manipulation/instrumentation/results/contact_held_a_b_edge_trace.csv`.

## What remains staged

This does not reach the Eyerok fight or the initial hand contact from ordinary controller play.  The probe still shortens level travel, stages the boss scheduler, and repeatedly places Mario until retail collision reports the desired real hand ownership.  Those earlier writes remain the clean boss/contact reachability obligation.  Nor does the result produce a complete lower-route entry: the six positive steps put the highest hand top at Y `-943`, whereas the tunnel floor is Y `-562`.  A clean route still needs a stronger departure or another support, the correct wall and horizontal alignment, a hand-to-warp trajectory, and an Act-3 continuation.  Act 6 remains separate.

## Exhaustive schedule quotient

A literal brute force over controller frames cannot finish: analog values, arbitrary waiting, animation timers, RNG, and Float32 positions make the search tree effectively unbounded.  The new checked search instead records only source action boundaries, retains a self-loop for every possible wait, and permits both hands to take any source-shaped edge together.  That last choice deliberately over-approximates the real scheduler, so it may admit impossible pairings but cannot lose a schedule merely because the retail boss normally serializes it.  The finite quotient has `9` boss phases with `21` edges and `16` hand phases with `38` edges per hand.  Crossing those with the six candidate Mario effects gives `181,944` raw cases; because the seed result is independent of the three edge labels, Coq checks the six distinct effect representatives and proves that every raw tuple maps to one of them instead of constructing a huge duplicate term.

The six candidate effects are: leave Mario's vertical speed unchanged or lower it, carry him as a platform without changing it, preserve it through Eyerok's zero-damage knockback, clear it on a hit from below, replace it with `20` for the checked held-A jump-kick, or replace it with `30` for Eyerok's ordinary top bounce.  The generated US and JP initializers fix Eyerok's hitbox at damage `0`, health `4`, and ordinary `INTERACT_BOUNCE_TOP`; its behavior script does not install the twirl-bounce subtype that would select the generic `80` branch.  Coq proves both that every fresh installer in this alphabet is at most `30` and that an arbitrary-length list of these effects preserves the bound `seed ≤ 31`.  Repeating a represented boss cycle therefore cannot itself create the first useful seed `32`; a larger seed must already enter through a separately authenticated predecessor or a live effect outside the classification.  The remaining proof bridge is to classify every reached clean Clight/retail frame as one of these six effects—this is not inferred merely from the action name.

The schedule quotient by itself does **not** prove that no dynamic intermediate support exists, because action names alone do not contain transformed triangles, X/Y/Z, update order, or Mario's selected floor owner.  It identifies seven action classes that can create a new upward hand maximum: sleep/wake's sine curve, idle's boss-wake offset, target-Mario's rise, begin-double's formation, double-pound's launch, attacked's `30` launch, and die's `50` launch.  Open, show-eye, close, smash, fist-push, fist-sweep, recover, become-active, and retreat remain timing or support states but do not create a higher hand maximum by themselves.

## Promoted dynamic-support barrier

Those seven classes were not seven untouched geometry searches.  The archived Eyerok proof already contained a generous two-hand barrier, and `proofs/EyerokRank15DynamicSupport.v` now reconstructs it in the active project.  Its generated-AST census checks every direct `oVelY` and `oGravity` assignment in the attack helper and all sixteen hand handlers in both versions: the only positive vertical writes are `30`, `50`, and `100`, and the relevant gravity writes are `0`, `-4`, `-15`, and `-20`.  The resulting complete positive rises are `98`, `288`, and `285`, so the proof grants every positive episode a common `288`-unit budget.  It also parses all `176` static Area-3 triangles and proves every upward one tops out at or below Y `384`, parses all `54` vertices across the four Eyerok hand collision meshes in each version and proves their local Y maximum is `338`, and checks the hand's `1.5` scale, giving the exact `507`-unit surface offset.

The barrier is deliberately more favorable than the real game.  The earlier-updated hand may use any arena floor at or below `-1150`, any checked direct pose, any partial rise up to the full `288` budget, and arbitrary stuttering.  The later-updated hand additionally receives every upward static floor in Area 3 and may land on the earlier hand's tallest mesh while retaining unused rise.  Induction then bounds the earlier origin at `-862`, its top at `-355`, the later origin at `672`, and the later top at `1179`.  Even granting Mario another `630` units above that top reaches only `1809`, below the `1889` minimum query height for the Area-2 floor at Y `1967`.  This exhausts the seven classes **inside the promoted relation**; repeating them no longer constitutes seven separate open searches.

The arbitrary projector has now been removed from the active capstone.  [`EyerokRank15LiveProjection.v`](../../proofs/EyerokRank15LiveProjection.v) checks from the generated US and JP ASTs that the boss requests the left hand (`side=-1`, model `88`) before the right hand (`side=1`, model `89`), and that the shared movement helper performs three distinct stores in the order `oVelY`, optional `oVelY` clamp, then `oPosY`.  That last fact exposed a real defect in the former bridge: one complete envelope transition per Clight micro-step cannot faithfully represent a movement whose velocity and position are stored at different steps.  The replacement uses nonempty connected chunks of actual `Clight.step2` steps and exact endpoint `Mem.load`s for each hand's X/Y/Z pose, velocity, gravity, action, floor height and pointer, behavior, active flags, and list links.  It also reads the floor surface's owner at offset `44`, follows a finite path from the live SURFACE-list head, requires the left-to-right adjacency while both hands remain live, proves every observed byte of the two distinct `608`-byte slots is disjoint, and makes deletion absorbing so a reused slot fails identity rather than reviving the old hand.  The accepted wrapper additionally requires a concrete step path from the selected program's real task-entry state, preventing a fabricated raw Clight state from serving as the start.  Any constructed run through these chunks inherits the `1809 < 1889` barrier.

This does not yet construct the ordinary controller run or classify every reached chunk.  The generated-source receipt names three immediate outside-call roots rather than claiming an exhaustive transitive inventory: `cur_obj_play_sound_2` occurs on boss wake, `create_sound_spawner` occurs in the hand's die handler, and `sqrtf` occurs in standard movement, while their declarations are abstract in the owning generated units; the earlier task-entry/spawn prefix may add further reached externals and must be inventoried separately.  The selected-program replay must prove which calls are reached and give each reached call an exact hand-slot frame or checked allocation effect; `create_sound_spawner` is especially important because legitimate object allocation may write inside the object pool.  The first failed endpoint now has a concrete interpretation—an exact pose exceeding its envelope, a floor owner other than static/earlier hand, changed list links, an intervening write, behavior/active mismatch or same-slot reuse, an outside-call effect, or an unclassifiable before/after chunk—and is the counterexample candidate to test.  Until those chunks, the transitive inventory, and the needed frames are constructed, Rank 15 remains narrowed rather than globally disproved.

## Vertical-speed-conservation verdict

Vertical Speed Conservation does not supply the formerly quoted extra `243` units with any checked stock seed.  That `243` was only the difference between the ordinary floor-query threshold and the hand top after adding a second 60-unit jump-kick arc on paper; it was not a stackable gameplay operation.  The generated US and JP action setter writes jump-kick velocity `20` directly, so entering jump-kick replaces a conserved speed instead of adding another 60 units of ascent to it.

The formal bound is deliberately favorable to VSC.  It starts at the observed hand top Y `-943`, preserves the chosen seed perfectly, sums every positive frame under ordinary gravity `4`, and grants both the ledge check's `+160` floor-query offset and `find_floor`'s `78`-unit upward tolerance.  The tunnel floor can therefore be reached by this arithmetic only if Mario himself gets to at least Y `-800`:

| Conserved seed | Full positive ascent | Mario's best Y | Effective query ceiling | Result against floor Y `-562` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `20` (jump-kick replacement) | `60` | `-883` | `-645` | misses by `83` |
| `26` (standard VSC26 candidate) | `98` | `-845` | `-607` | misses by `45` |
| `30` (full ordinary bounce seed) | `128` | `-815` | `-577` | misses by `15` |
| `31` | `136` | `-807` | `-569` | misses by `7` |
| `32` | `144` | `-799` | `-561` | clears the vertical arithmetic threshold by `1` |

The theorem proves that every integral seed at or below `31` misses, not just the rows sampled in the table.  Seed `32` is only the first arithmetic threshold: it does not prove that stock play can create and preserve that seed, that Mario encounters the required wall while descending, that the ledge floor is selected at the needed X/Z, or that the subsequent warp route works.  The complete generated Area-3 collision initializer supplies one more useful exclusion: across all `176` static triangles in both selected versions, no positively oriented face spans the open Y band from the arena's highest static support at `-1150` to the tunnel floor at `-562`.  Mario therefore cannot bank the jump-kick or VSC height on an intermediate *static* floor in that band; a second hand or another dynamic support remains a separate escape.

The checked Coq developments are [`EyerokRank15VSC.v`](../../proofs/EyerokRank15VSC.v), [`EyerokRank15ScheduleSearch.v`](../../proofs/EyerokRank15ScheduleSearch.v), [`EyerokRank15DynamicSupport.v`](../../proofs/EyerokRank15DynamicSupport.v), and [`EyerokRank15LiveProjection.v`](../../proofs/EyerokRank15LiveProjection.v).  They use generated game-source initializers and action bodies for their constants, action graph, geometry, store order, and memory offsets; [Ukikipedia's Vertical Speed Conservation page](https://ukikipedia.net/wiki/Vertical_Speed_Conservation) is useful background for the technique and the conventional “VSC26” name, but is not a proof premise.
