# High unloaded-top shell mounting with delayed cancellation

14 November 2026. **The tested delayed shell route does not produce a usable
Ink handoff.** It really keeps Mario high, obtains the fresh low floor cache,
and allows later actor pushes. The missing part is the action: native shell
mounting without immediate Z becomes backward ground knockback, whose
handler does not align Mario to the cached floor. Later Z/B/analog inputs
do not turn that state into the needed ground slide. A matched early control
confirms that natural recovery still exits through freefall while the top
is live; this failure is not merely recovery happening after the top explodes.

This is a bounded native JP Wafel result, not a global impossibility proof,
original-ROM replay or clean no-new-A route. It focuses on actual high
unloaded-top states, **not** a low landing on the rotating top. The source
guards and existing Coq scope are documented in
[the high-entry source report](ink-high-entry-source.md). The complete
experiment record is [ink-high-delayed-shell-receipt.json](ink-high-delayed-shell-receipt.json).

## Exact high-ground-state near miss

M is `MarioState.pos`, C is raw Mario-object collision position, and D is
stored `header.gfx.pos`. D does not specify the visible model/render matrix.
Use the phase 128, neutral-suffix, box-before 130 recipe at H=25000:

| Boundary | M=C | D | Action / cache / platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial cut before 128 | `(-1950,25000,-939)` | Same | Supplied FREEFALL; no supplied cache/platform |
| After128 mounting update | `(-1950,25000,-939)` | `(-1950,25045,-939)` | Native BACKWARD_GROUND_KB; floor 768, static owner, no platform |
| After129 native free-shell push | `(-1951.5792236328125,25000,-1025.0386962890625)` | Same | BACKWARD_GROUND_KB; floor 768, no platform |
| After130 supplied-box contact | `(-1946.240234375,25000,-1037.084716796875)` | Same | BACKWARD_GROUND_KB; floor 768, no platform |

The source ordering explains this concrete behavior. On the first update,
the shell interaction changes FREEFALL to RIDING_SHELL_GROUND. Without a
fresh Z cancel, the high stopped ground step selects BACKWARD_GROUND_KB.
That change does not execute the newly selected knockback handler until the
next update. The shell-riding handler's graphics adjustment accounts for the
initial stored D.Y being H+45; it does not move raw collision down to the warp.
On the next updates the native free shell and then box really push M.
The box is in the recorded collision list at130. Thus this is not a failure
to register the later actor, nor an assumption that knockback cannot move.

The exact H=67277 counterpart has the same X/Z values and cache 768. After
mounting M=C.Y is 67277 while D.Y is 67322; after the two pushes all three
positions keep Y=67277. Top collision remains unloaded. Raw collision never
contacts the real low warp, and no high-to-low alignment gap is produced.

`common_ground_knockback_action` executes `perform_ground_step` but never
calls `align_with_floor`. Merely seeing a grounded action ID at high Y is
therefore insufficient. None of the 128 recipes executes the high crawling,
crouch-slide, butt-slide, stomach-slide or dive-slide action handlers. Held
and freshly pulsed Z/B inputs, including B+Z and analog, do not supply a
generic knockback cancellation. No claim about entering their common helper
is inferred from a superficially similar action ID or animation.

## Recovery before the relevant top phase

The phase 126/128/129 menu reaches normal knockback recovery only after the
original handoff timing. One matched earlier control addresses that limit
without setting an animation frame or top timer. The native prefix reaches
phase 94; the shell box action is supplied there, and the high airborne
Mario/shell cut is made before 97. Use H=25000, late Z pulses, and the same
box XYZ cut before 130. No Mario writes follow the initial cut.

| Native update after initial cut | Top timer afterward | Observed state |
|---|---:|---|
|31|128|BACKWARD_GROUND_KB; M=C=D=`(-1951.578857421875,25000,-1025.021240234375)`; cache 768; no platform |
|32|129|Native animation ends and selects IDLE at exactly the same high position/cache |
|33|130|IDLE exits to FREEFALL at the same high position/cache |
|34|131|Box cut plus newly pressed Z produces high GROUND_POUND; M=C=`(-1946.240234375,25000,-1037.084716796875)`; D retains the preceding high XYZ |

The top is still active (`activeFlags=257`) at all these boundaries. The
ordinary idle handler checks OFF_FLOOR before ABOVE_SLIDE, walking, B or Z.
At raw Y=25000 above fresh floor 768, the named recovery therefore leaves the
grounded path. The subsequent Z is processed as an airborne ground-pound
input. Ground-pound is not a ground slide and does not perform the needed
floor alignment. The early control directly removes the “top exploded
before recovery” explanation, while retaining the exact OFF_FLOOR failure.
Its box contact occurs after IDLE has already exited, so it is not evidence
about every possible interaction that might replace IDLE beforehand.

## Scope, proofs and next step

The finite menu and the existing source/Coq guard result agree on this
named branch: a high shell mount without immediate cancellation leads to
ground knockback; ordinary native recovery through IDLE cannot supply the
required slide while OFF_FLOOR remains set and no earlier interaction
replaces the action. The current guarded proof's assumptions are preserved;
this runtime study adds no Coq theorem and performs no Coq rebuild. It also
adds no original-ROM replay, actor-delivery result or complete controller
route. Only the final late-menu output is counted; its rerun checked the
same menu after adding reusable early-root selection.

The closest verified state already has high M, fresh floor 768 and a real
later actor shove. Its first missing condition is **execution of a floor-
aligning ground-action handler**, not lack of height or lack of collision.
The next useful question is an action-changing interaction before the idle
OFF_FLOOR check or a different native high-air conversion that leaves the
later box handler reachable in the same update. A larger delayed-Z grid on
ordinary knockback is unlikely to answer that question. Earlier-interaction
source constraints are recorded separately in the high-entry source note;
they must not be generalized to all engine histories or cloned behaviors.
