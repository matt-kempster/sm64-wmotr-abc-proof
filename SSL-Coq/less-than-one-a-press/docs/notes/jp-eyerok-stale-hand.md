# Original-JP stale Eyerok-hand address audit

## Current verdict

The stale-hand route is now disproved within the audited stock source-shaped model.  On the authentic original-JP ROM, retaining either sleeping-hand address reaches the real first Area-2 platform-apply call only after that address has become a motionless `bhvStaticObject`, so Mario moves by exactly `(0,0,0)`.  A destroyed hand can instead target allocation 53 or 54, but the complete deletion-only suffix has only one effective replacement, Spindel, and its exact ordinary effect moves Mario about 8 down and 38 backward.  More importantly, the ordinary pointer cannot be installed: fist-push remains at the low home height (and both checked central cases are also edge-stopped short of the warp), one-hand SHOW_EYE reaches its X/Z with an open top at only Y=`-1027`, its strongest lethal continuation peaks at Y=`-739`, every rising target/double-pound family remains horizontally short, the sibling cannot supply a later eligible surface, and the complete post-Mario position-writer census has no Area-3 writer.  A freed but unreused dead-hand slot is also an identity payload after its 25-frame ground return finishes before the 40-frame deletion.  The separate PU installation remains disproved by its support, transport, and dialog-lifecycle bounds.  What remains is a linked Clight/retail refinement of those source-shaped classifications, or a concrete failed premise; out-of-bounds writes and post-undefined-behavior execution are separate machine-level questions.

## Floor/platform mismatch and stock installation verdict

The Area-3 instant warp is static collision, with exact vertices `(-191,286,-1222)`, `(-191,384,-1023)`, `(192,384,-1023)`, and `(192,286,-1222)`.  A coherent query on that floor stores a null platform, which is what the natural machine run observes.  The desired pre-warp state instead needs Mario's cached floor to remain this static warp while the final fresh floor query stores one of the hand addresses.  The two previously known Eyerok Pedro configurations still cannot do this: their hand pivots remain at home Z=`-3393`, and even the coarse hand envelope ends at Z=`-2934`, far behind the warp's Z=`[-1222,-1023]`.

There is distinct conditional Pedro geometry at the warp itself.  At the central sample `(0,-1100)`, the complete static collision census finds exactly two downward faces, at Y=`-409` and Y=`768`.  Because the source Pedro branch requires a positive gap from 2 through 160, the selected hand floor must be in `[-569,-411]` under the low ceiling or `[608,766]` under the high ceiling.  For the closed mesh, whose top is `306` above its pivot, those become pivot bands `[-875,-717]` and `[302,460]`; for the open mesh, whose top is `507` above its pivot, the low band becomes `[-1076,-918]`.  A closed hand injected on the recorded warp floor would have pivot Y=`346.08044`, top Y=`652.08044`, and ceiling gap `115.919556`; landing there would take the Pedro branch, retain the older static warp floor and old X/Z, snap Y to the hand top, and let the final query save the hand.  This remains valid conditional geometry, but the new stock-pose audit proves the game cannot place a hand there.

The matching-ROM ordinary-install probe follows four disclosed cases from accepted level select through Area 3.  Both conditional FIST_PUSH bodies retain closed collision at pivot Y=`-1534`; the right and left families stop with conservative maximum collision Z=`-1409.595459` and `-1434`, behind the warp's nearest Z=`-1222`.  In contrast, both naturally scheduled one-hand OPEN-to-SHOW_EYE cases cross the full warp Z interval and finish near Z=`-892`, but remain at pivot Y=`-1534`, so their open top is only Y=`-1027` and their later closed top is Y=`-1228`.  The exact source-shaped lethal `50/-4` arc has 25 integrations to the ground and a maximum relative rise of `288`, putting the open top at Y=`-739`, still `170` below the low floor band.  TARGET_MARIO reaches pivot Y=`-1234` but its closed top is only Y=`-928` and its conservative Z envelope ends at `-1484`; double-pound's boss-clamped envelope ends at `-1634`.  Together with the pinned-source action classification, these cases yield the exhaustive stock split used by the Coq theorem: a hand pose is horizontally remote from the warp, or its relevant floor is at or below Y=`-739`, and either side contradicts the joint installation condition.

The scheduler and writer residuals do not add another installer.  FIST_PUSH's other hand can only open at home in a two-hand phase; a forward one-hand SHOW_EYE phase has no live sibling, and the boss's exclusive exposure latch prevents a delayed second surface from changing that split.  Surface hands update before PLAYER, Mario's action then copies MarioState to the Mario object, and SSL Area 3 contains only the later GENACTOR boss plus an empty macro list.  Across the pinned game source, the only named `set_mario_pos` callers are platform displacement, Dorrie, and the tilting inverted pyramid, while the only direct raw-Mario-object position writer is the butterfly; platform displacement runs earlier and the three actor behaviors are absent from Area 3.  There is consequently no defined stock post-copy writer between the Pedro decision and final platform query that can move the Object sample to a different hand pose.

PU surface availability is now split exhaustively at the stock scheduler and lifecycle boundaries.  At the chosen three-negative-Z-period sample Mario's raw Z is `-197708`, while a hand placed at the local warp would be at Z=`-1100`; the exact separation is `196608`, far beyond the strict `1000`-unit collision-load distance.  Moving the hand is not a stock escape: every Area-3 static vertex lies in Z=`[-3954,-255]`, all four hand meshes have local X/Z magnitude at most `153`, and even granting a second hand the full conservative yaw-independent `459`-unit mesh extension leaves both hands in Z=`[-4413,204]`; the hand movement helper rejects a destination with no floor, the only direct Z assignments retreat to home or interpolate toward the boss-clamped `[-3293,-2093]` band, target/push motion is at most `50`, and the only growing-speed sweep is exactly sideways.  The next signed-16 support copy is `[-69949,-65332]`, leaving a `60919`-unit open gap.  In an ordinary frame, the source order is clear surfaces, update/load hands, apply the previous platform, update Mario, then query his new platform; even granting `50` helpful units from an earlier platform, loading near an ordinary hand and ending at `-197708` requires Mario's own Z contribution to be at most `-195559`.  Although JP's unfixed dialog helper can retain a dynamic partition while an airborne Mario remains scheduled, Eyerok never offers that scheduler state with the required surface: its intro is the only dialog before the fight and both hands are then idle at home Z=`-3393`, whose generous collision envelope ends at `-2934`, while its only later dialog starts 60 frames into the zero-hand death state and each dying hand's 40-frame animation has already deleted it; Area 3 contains only the boss and an empty macro list.  Therefore neither dialog window can retain a hand over the warp, and the previously identified airborne `60919`-gap question is unreachable for this installation rather than an unfinished motion bound.  Together with the ordinary-frame threshold and the stock hand-support invariant, this disproves clean PU installation in the selected in-bounds model.

The four hand collision initializers are now checked directly.  Every local X/Z coordinate lies within `[-153,153]`; the earlier draft's `151` bound missed two X=`153` open-hand vertices.  At scale 1.5, a yaw-independent offset bound is therefore 459.  For the target-Mario advance subcase, a supplied pivot premise `Z <= -1943` keeps the hand envelope at or behind `-1484`, strictly away from the warp's nearest Z=`-1222`.  This is only a conditional subcase: fist-push/sweep can advance farther toward Mario, so the theorem is not a universal ordinary-scale mismatch exclusion.

## Ordinary-scale payload and installation split

Destroying the first hand conditionally leaves 53 live allocations ahead of its freed cell, so its Area-2 replacement ordinal is 54; destroying the last hand leaves 52 ahead, so its ordinal is 53.  The authenticated baseline Area-2 run has 83 allocations.  Under the deliberately generous deletion-only model, suppressing earlier entries can therefore select only a baseline allocation from the inclusive suffix 53 through 83, or suppress so many objects that the stale cell is not reused.  A fresh retail census checks all 31 suffix entries against the exact fields consumed by `apply_platform_displacement`: platform X/Z velocity and pitch/yaw/roll angular velocity.  Every suffix payload is an identity for those fields except allocation 64, Spindel; the moving pyramid walls at 60 through 63 have vertical object velocity, but the displacement function does not add platform Y velocity to Mario.  At the recorded ordinary center, the compiled binary32 proof evaluates Spindel's result as bits for `(0,338.5134,-1138.419)`, integer projection `(0,338,-1138)`, from `(0,346.08044,-1100)`.  The four exact warp vertices likewise map to integer Y values `281` or `374` and Z values `-1261` or `-1060`, confirming that the ordinary lever arm supplies a small downward/backward perturbation rather than the PU lift.  The no-reuse case is now classified too: allocation initially zeros every angular-velocity field, Eyerok never writes those fields, and the lethal arc grounds on integration 25, after which DIE clears forward speed and the common movement tail recomputes X/Z velocity as zero for the remaining 15 animation frames before deletion.  Thus a genuinely unreused cell is also an identity payload.

The ordinary installation residual is closed in the audited stock model by the pose split, sibling schedule, and post-copy writer census above.  Because no clean stock execution can save the hand pointer at the warp, the small Spindel down/back perturbation no longer needs a downstream no-A usefulness proof for this route: it is a conditional counterfactual retained for testing only if a linked execution violates one of the installation premises.  The PU-scale branch remains separately closed because no fresh-load, stock hand-travel, or dialog-retained installation survives.  A whole-program conclusion still requires showing that every real linked CompCert frame refines these source-shaped classes; a concrete failure of that refinement would identify the exact pose, writer, sibling, or lifetime event to reopen.

The first PU arithmetic witness, raw `(0,262491,10484660)` with signed-16 query `(0,347,-1100)`, remains a valid collision alias and makes Spindel reach Act-3 height, but it cannot itself install a platform: its four extra Y periods put raw Mario Y more than 229000 units from every signed-16 dynamic floor, while `update_mario_platform` requires a gap below four.  It is now retained only as a possible State/Object-split witness.  The coherent stale-hand branch instead uses no Y period and three negative Z periods.  Its floor-snapped Mario input is `(0,652.08044,-197708)`, whose collision Z is still `-1100`; this point is both close to the hand floor and far enough from Spindel's pivot to provide a useful lever arm.  Every conversion remains finite and within the selected defined CompCert integer-conversion range, so this branch does not rely on an out-of-bounds write or post-undefined-behavior continuation.

## Alternate lifecycle and Spindel payload

When an Eyerok hand finishes dying, its source calls create 20 mist objects, 30 triangle fragments, and one sound spawner before the hand is unloaded.  Those 51 live allocations, plus the boss, sit ahead of the last hand on the free list, so the last hand is conditionally Area-2 allocation 53; if the sibling hand is still alive, the first destroyed hand is conditionally allocation 54.  This is a source-audited ordinal calculation, not yet one continuous machine replay of a hand death and warp.

The authenticated Area-2 census contains 83 ordinary allocations and identifies Spindel as allocation 64 with position bits `(c519a000,4503f412,c4b22000)`, Z-velocity bits `40a00000` (`5.0f`), current pitch `256`, and pitch velocity `256`.  An allocation-identity fixture sets the no-respawn bytes of 10 macro records and obtains 73 allocations with Spindel at 54; setting 11 obtains 72 allocations with Spindel at 53.  Suppression is no longer merely an injected-byte idea: the exact Area-2 initializer contains fifteen individual collectible yellow coins—nine `macro_yellow_coin_1` and six `macro_yellow_coin_2`—and neither behavior has the persistent-respawn flag.  A normal coin interaction marks the coin, its loop deletes it, end-of-frame unload ORs `0xFF00` into that macro record's 16-bit respawn cell, and the next Area-2 load skips that allocation.  The Coq inventory selects concrete ten- and eleven-record subsets, so the stock source contains enough legitimate omissions for either stale ordinal.  What remains unproved is one zero-A controller trajectory which collects a chosen ten or eleven before entering Area 3 and preserves the rest of the proposed setup; the proof does not equate inventory with reachability.

## Act-3 continuation

The ordinary sleeping-hand first apply starts below Y=347 and adds no displacement, so it cannot help with Act 3.  The coherent three-period Spindel apply instead leaves Mario above the star at Y=`5469.4233` and at raw X/Z `(0,-197679.7)`, whereas the star is at `(500,5050,-500)` and object interaction uses raw coordinates rather than signed-16 collision aliases.  The integer-truncated Z gap is exactly `197179`; the four Area-2 warp objects are at Z=`6451`, `256`, `2900`, and `-2647`, so even the nearest is `195032` units away.  Both Area-2/Area-3 instant-warp displacements are `(0,0,0)` and therefore preserve the bad raw coordinate.  A normal warp or death reload would reinitialize Mario at an ordinary spawn, but that discards the stale displacement and returns to the already-known elevator/pole gates rather than converting it into star contact.  Consequently a positive continuation needs another clean raw transport on essentially the same scale as the installation, followed by the controlled fall and star interaction; the exact rotation itself neither reanchors nor collects.  Eyerok still supplies no Act-6 continuation.
